The morning subway rush

Skill
Reading
Time
About 20 minutes
Done when
You have written what the pronouns point to, answered three questions, and written one change

Omar rides the subway in the morning rush; coworker Jia is in the same car. Track it, them, and they. Cars, bags, and announcements are pointed back to. Write in the box on this page. It stays in this browser for this visit, and it is not uploaded.

Material

About 240 words. Look up at most one unknown word. There is no word list and no full translation.

At seven forty, Omar swipes into the subway station. Jia waves from the platform near the map. They board the same car and hold the poles. The car fills at the next stop. Omar lifts his bag higher so it does not hit anyone. Jia checks two messages and replies to them. A recorded voice asks riders to move inside. Omar hears it twice and steps back. They reach the transfer hall after four stops. Jia points to the green line signs. Omar follows them down the stairs. On the second train a seat opens. Jia takes it and offers to hold Omar’s folder. He keeps it on his lap instead. The doors stay open longer than usual. Omar watches them and counts the seconds. Jia stands when their office stop appears. They exit with a slow crowd. Outside Omar buys a bottle of water and drinks it. Jia waits while he caps it. They walk the last block together. Omar unlocks the office gate with his card. Jia holds it open for a delivery cart. Inside they hang their coats. Omar puts his bag under the desk. It stays there until lunch. Jia sends a short note that the train was packed. Omar answers it after he opens his laptop. The morning rush is already fading outside. They start the first meeting on time.

Steps

  1. Read once. In Chinese, say what the main people did.
  2. Read again. For each it, them, and they, write what it points to.
  3. Answer the three questions below. Write first, then open the check.

One more round

After the three answers, tap these words back into the order of the line. About half a minute. Finish before you open the check.

Open after you write
  • so it does not hit / keeps it / drinks it / caps it / It stays / answers it: bag, folder, bottle, card, note — use the nearest noun
  • replies to them / follows them / watches them: messages / signs / doors
  • They board / They reach / They exit / They walk / They start: Omar and Jia

Three questions

  1. Omar’s bag.
  2. The green line signs.
  3. Jia’s short note.

One note for Chinese speakers

them may point to messages, signs, or doors. Each time, go back to the nearest plural noun.

Keep one change

After the check, write only one thing to change next time.

  • If you took “follows them” as the crowd, next time look at green line signs. Change only that.
  • If they were right, write this: next time you see them or it, still find the noun first, and do not switch to copying new words.

Writing both means no focus. Keep one.
