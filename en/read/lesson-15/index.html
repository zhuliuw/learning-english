A fire drill at the office

Skill
Reading
Time
About 20 minutes
Done when
You have written what the pronouns point to, answered three questions, and written one change

During an office fire drill, Helen leads her team downstairs; guard Chen counts at the exit. Track it, them, and they. Alarms, lists, and floors are pointed back to. Write in the box on this page. It stays in this browser for this visit, and it is not uploaded.

Material

About 240 words. Look up at most one unknown word. There is no word list and no full translation.

At ten sharp the fire alarm starts on the fifth floor. Helen closes her laptop and picks up the team list. She folds it once and leads three coworkers to the stairwell. They keep to the right and do not use the elevator. Chen stands at the ground exit with a clipboard. He checks names and marks them. Helen hands him the list and waits. A manager counts the groups and waves them farther from the door. Helen moves her team under a tree. They watch the building while the drill continues. Chen radios the fifth floor and confirms it is clear. Helen hears it on the speaker nearby. Rain starts, so she opens a small umbrella and shares it. Two coworkers step under it. Chen calls the all-clear after twelve minutes. They walk back in a single line. Helen returns the list to the desk drawer. It stays there for the next drill. Chen posts a short note on the board. Helen reads it and tells the team the timing was fine. One coworker asks about the stair lights. Chen says they will be checked after lunch. Helen unlocks her laptop and saves a draft. She sends it to her manager before noon. The alarm stays quiet for the rest of the morning. Helen drinks water and stretches her shoulders. Chen collects the clipboards and stacks them. Helen thanks him and returns to her seat. The drill is over, but the list still sits in the drawer. She checks it once more before the afternoon meeting.

Steps

  1. Read once. In Chinese, say what the main people did.
  2. Read again. For each it, them, and they, write what it points to.
  3. Answer the three questions below. Write first, then open the check.

One more round

After the three answers, tap these words back into the order of the line. About half a minute. Finish before you open the check.

Open after you write
  • folds it / confirms it / shares it / It stays / reads it / sends it / checks it: list, clear floor, umbrella, note, draft, and so on
  • marks them / waves them / stacks them / they will be checked: names / groups / clipboards / stair lights
  • They keep / They watch / They walk: Helen and the coworkers

Three questions

  1. The team list.
  2. The names.
  3. The draft she saved.

One note for Chinese speakers

they sometimes points to people, sometimes to stair lights. Look at the verb: walk and watch usually mean people; will be checked usually means things.

Keep one change

After the check, write only one thing to change next time.

  • If you took “sends it” as the list, next time look at saves a draft. Change only that.
  • If they were right, write this: next time you see them or it, still find the noun first, and do not switch to copying new words.

Writing both means no focus. Keep one.
