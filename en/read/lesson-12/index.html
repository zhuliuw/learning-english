Reading · Lesson 12 · about 20 minutes

A delivery downstairs

Skill
Reading
Time
About 20 minutes
Done when
You have written what the pronouns point to, answered three questions, and written one change

Dan goes downstairs for a delivery; neighbor Sue is at the locker too. Track it, them, and they. Packages, a text, and lockers are pointed back to. Write in the box on this page. It stays in this browser for this visit, and it is not uploaded. Reading stops at this lesson.

Material

This is about picking up a delivery, about 240 words. Look up at most one unknown word. There is no word list and no full translation.

On Monday evening, Dan gets a text that a package is in the lobby locker. He reads it twice and puts on his shoes. Sue is already at the locker wall with two bags. They say hello and wait for a free screen. Dan types his code and opens a small door. The package inside is heavier than he expected. He lifts it with both hands. Sue opens another door and takes out a thin envelope. She checks them against her phone list. Dan closes his door and hears it click. A delivery worker rolls in a cart with more boxes. He stacks them under the stairs. Dan asks whether the large shelf is full. The worker nods and says a second cart is coming. Sue holds the lobby door for Dan. They walk to the elevator together. On the third floor Dan gets off. Sue stays on for two more floors. Dan sets the package on the kitchen table. He opens it and finds a new keyboard. He keeps the box for recycling. Sue knocks later and asks to borrow tape. Dan lends it and she returns it in ten minutes. He folds the empty mailer and drops it in the paper bin. The text from the locker app stays on his phone. He deletes it after he checks the order page. Outside the window a second cart arrives. Dan hears neighbors talking in the hallway. He washes his hands and plugs in the keyboard. It works on the first try. He sends a short thank-you note to the seller.

Steps

  1. Read once. In Chinese, say what the main people did.
  2. Read again. For each it, them, and they, write what it points to.
  3. Answer the three questions below. Write first, then open the check.

Three questions

Write all three below before you open the check. It stays in this browser for this visit. It is not uploaded, and it is not scored.

Write a little for all three, then open the check.

One more round

After the three answers, tap these words back into the order of the line. About half a minute. Finish before you open the folded check.

Open after you write
  • reads it / deletes it: the text; lifts it / opens it: the package; lends it / returns it: tape; It works: the keyboard
  • checks them / stacks them: Sue’s items or boxes on the cart
  • They say hello / They walk: Dan and Sue

Three questions

  1. The locker text message.
  2. What Sue took out (bags/envelope against her list).
  3. The tape.

One note for Chinese speakers

it shifts quickly here: text, package, door, tape, keyboard. At each spot, use the nearest singular noun.

Keep one change

After the check, write only one thing to change next time.

  • If you took “lends it” as the keyboard, next time look back to borrow tape. Change only that.
  • If all were right, keep this change: next time you see them or it, still find the noun first; do not switch to copying new words.

Writing both means no focus. Keep one.

What this lesson does not practice

Not listening, not speaking, and not a word list. Writing lives under Writing, not here.
