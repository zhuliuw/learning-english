The weekend wet market

Skill
Reading
Time

On Saturday morning, Suki goes to the wet market; stall owner Old Ma knows her. Track it, them, and they. Vegetables, bags, and change are pointed back to. Write in the box on this page. It stays in this browser for this visit, and it is not uploaded.

Material

About 240 words. Look up at most one unknown word. There is no word list and no full translation.

On Saturday morning, Suki walks to the wet market with two cloth bags. Old Ma waves from the greens stall. They talk about the rain while he weighs spinach. Suki points to the tomatoes and asks for six. He picks them and sets them in a bowl. She pays and drops the change into her pocket. It jingles when she walks. At the fish stall a seller wraps two fillets. Suki smells them and nods. She puts them in the colder bag. Old Ma holds a bunch of scallions for her. She takes it and thanks him. They finish before the aisle grows crowded. Suki checks her list and crosses items off. She folds it into the bag. A neighbor asks where the tofu is. Suki points and walks with her. They buy one block and split it at home later. Outside Suki balances both bags on her bike. One strap slips, so she tightens it. Old Ma calls that the spinach is especially fresh. Suki waves and rides home. In the kitchen she rinses the greens and drains them. She sets the fish in the fridge and closes it. The list on the counter is blank now. She throws it away after dinner planning. Her roommate asks about the tomatoes. Suki says they will go into a salad. She washes her hands and hangs the bags to dry. They drip on the balcony for an hour.

Steps

  1. Read once. In Chinese, say what the main people did.
  2. Read again. For each it, them, and they, write what it points to.
  3. Answer the three questions below. Write first, then open the check.

One more round

After the three answers, tap these words back into the order of the line. About half a minute. Finish before you open the check.

Open after you write
  • It jingles / takes it / folds it / tightens it / closes it / throws it: change, scallions, list, strap, fridge, list
  • picks them / smells them / puts them / drains them / They drip: tomatoes / fish / greens / bags
  • They talk / They finish / They buy / they will go: Suki with Old Ma or the neighbor; or the tomatoes

Three questions

  1. The change in her pocket.
  2. The two fillets.
  3. The tomatoes.

One note for Chinese speakers

The last they points to the tomatoes, not people. Look at go into a salad.

Keep one change

After the check, write only one thing to change next time.

  • If you took the last they as the roommate, next time look at asks about the tomatoes. Change only that.
  • If they were right, write this: next time you see them or it, still find the noun first, and do not switch to copying new words.

Writing both means no focus. Keep one.
