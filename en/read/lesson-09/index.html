Reading · Lesson 9 · about 20 minutes

An hour at the bank

Skill
Reading
Time
About 20 minutes
Done when
You have written what the pronouns point to, answered three questions, and written one change

After work, Min goes to the bank for a card; her coworker Wei waits with her. Track it, them, and they. The ticket, forms, and card are pointed back to, but not as one thing. Write in the box on this page. It stays in this browser for this visit, and it is not uploaded.

Material

This is about an hour at the bank, about 240 words. Look up at most one unknown word. There is no word list and no full translation.

After work on Tuesday, Min walks to the bank two blocks from her office. Wei comes with her because he needs to deposit a check. They take a number from the machine by the door. Min holds the ticket and reads it twice. A clerk calls their number after twenty minutes. Min sits down and opens her ID. The clerk asks for two forms and a photo. Min fills them at the side table. Wei waits on the bench and checks his phone. The clerk takes the forms and stamps them. She says the card will be ready in ten minutes. Min and Wei walk to the water cooler. They talk about a report due on Friday. When the clerk waves, Min goes back to the desk. The clerk hands her a thin envelope. Min opens it and finds the new card inside. She signs the receipt and puts it in her bag. Wei finishes his deposit and joins her at the door. They leave before the bank closes. Outside, Min checks the card one more time. It shows her name in clear letters. Wei asks whether she will use it for lunch pay. Min says yes and slides it into her wallet. They walk back toward the subway. On the platform Min texts her sister that the card is done. Wei buys a bottle of water from a machine. He offers it to Min, but she declines. The train arrives and they find two seats together. Min keeps the envelope folded in her bag until she gets home. She puts the card on the fridge so she will not forget to activate it. The evening feels quieter after the bank.

Steps

  1. Read once. In Chinese, say what the main people did.
  2. Read again. For each it, them, and they, write what it points to.
  3. Answer the three questions below. Write first, then open the check.

Three questions

One more round

After the three answers, tap these words back into the order of the line. About half a minute. Finish before you open the folded check.

Open after you write
  • reads it / opens it / activates it: ticket, envelope’s card, or new card — use the nearest singular noun
  • fills them / stamps them: the two forms
  • They take / They talk / they find: Min and Wei

Three questions

  1. The ticket (number slip).
  2. The two forms.
  3. The bottle of water.

One note for Chinese speakers

them is plural. Here it is the forms. Find the nearest plural noun. Do not treat every it as the same card.

Keep one change

After the check, write only one thing to change next time.

  • If you took “fills them” as the card, next time look back to two forms. Change only that.
  • If all were right, keep this change: next time you see them or it, still find the noun first; do not switch to copying new words.

Writing both means no focus. Keep one.
