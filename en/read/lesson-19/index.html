Renewing the lease

Skill
Reading
Time
About 20 minutes
Done when
You have written what the pronouns point to, answered three questions, and written one change

Before the lease ends, Ira meets landlord Ms. Cho to renew. Track it, them, and they. Contracts, keys, and terms are pointed back to. Write in the box on this page. It stays in this browser for this visit, and it is not uploaded.

Material

About 240 words. Look up at most one unknown word. There is no word list and no full translation.

On a Thursday evening, Ira meets Ms. Cho in the lobby to renew the lease. Ms. Cho brings two printed copies and a pen. They sit at the small table by the mailboxes. Ira reads the rent line twice and circles it. Ms. Cho explains the new start date and points to it. Ira asks about the deposit terms. She answers them in short sentences. Ira signs both pages and initials them. Ms. Cho keeps one copy and returns the other. Ira folds it into his folder. They walk upstairs to check the spare keys. Ms. Cho finds two keys on a ring and tests them. Ira takes one and labels it. The other stays with her. In the hallway a neighbor asks how long the lease runs. Ira says it runs one more year. Ms. Cho posts a note about building paint next month. Ira reads it on the way out. He transfers the first month online that night. The bank app shows a confirmation and he saves it. Ms. Cho texts that the payment arrived. Ira replies and pins the message. It stays at the top of the chat. On Friday he files both the lease and the receipt. They sit in the same envelope. His roommate asks whether the rent changed. Ira shows the circled line and explains it. They agree to split utilities the same way. Ira hangs the spare key on a hook by the door. It is easy to find when guests visit.

Steps

  1. Read once. In Chinese, say what the main people did.
  2. Read again. For each it, them, and they, write what it points to.
  3. Answer the three questions below. Write first, then open the check.

Open after you write
  • circles it / points to it / folds it / labels it / it runs / reads it / saves it / pins It / explains it / key It: rent line, date, copy, key, lease, note, confirmation, message, and so on
  • answers them / initials them / tests them / They sit: terms / pages / keys / lease and receipt
  • They sit / They walk / They agree: Ira and Ms. Cho / the roommate

Three questions

  1. The rent line.
  2. Both pages.
  3. The lease.

One note for Chinese speakers

In it runs one more year, it points to the lease, not the rent line. The neighbor asked about the lease.

Keep one change

After the check, write only one thing to change next time.

  • If you took “it runs” as the rent line, next time look at how long the lease runs. Change only that.
  • If they were right, write this: next time you see them or it, still find the noun first, and do not switch to copying new words.

Writing both means no focus. Keep one.
