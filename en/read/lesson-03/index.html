Reading · Lesson 3 · about 15 minutes

Three work messages

Skill
Reading
Time
About 15 minutes
Done when
You have written what the pronouns point to, answered three questions, and written one change

This lesson adds one thing: what he, she, and it point back to. Do not mark every verb again. Write on paper. This page does not save what you write.

Passage

These are a few work messages before lunch. Look up at most one word. There is no word list, and do not translate the whole passage.

Before lunch, Chen Wei reads three work messages. His manager writes, "Please send the draft today." Chen Wei reads it and does not reply yet. At 11:20 his teammate Nora sends a note. She says she can check the numbers. He thanks her and sends the file to her. Nora finds one wrong date. She writes that the meeting is on Wednesday, not Tuesday. Chen Wei fixes the date. He sends the draft to his manager. The manager answers with one word: "Good." He does not ask for another change. Chen Wei closes the chat and goes to lunch.

Steps

  1. Read once. Say in Chinese what is going on. Do not translate sentence by sentence.
  2. Read again. Do one job: when you meet he, she, or it, write who or what it points back to. Mark her or his only if it blocks you. Do not turn that into another grammar task.
  3. Answer the three questions. Write them down before you open the check.

Three questions

  1. In “Chen Wei reads it”, what does it point to?
  2. In “He thanks her”, who is he, and who is her?
  3. In “He does not ask for another change”, who is he?
Write on paper first, then check
  • it in “reads it”: the manager’s line, “Please send the draft today.”
  • She / she: Nora
  • He thanks her: he is Chen Wei, her is Nora
  • He sends the draft: Chen Wei
  • He does not ask: the manager, not Chen Wei

The questions

  1. The manager’s sentence, “Please send the draft today.”
  2. he is Chen Wei, and her is Nora.
  3. The manager.

Keep one change

After you check, write down one thing you will do differently.

  • If you took the last he to be Chen Wei, next time look at the person named just before that he. Change only that.
  • If the pronouns were right, write this: next lesson, still ask only what a pronoun points to.

Writing both means you have no priority. Keep one.

What this lesson does not practice

Not listening, not speaking, and not copying words from the messages into a word list.
