Asking for annual leave

Skill
Reading
Time
About 20 minutes
Done when
You have written what the pronouns point to, answered three questions, and written one change

Eli asks his manager for annual leave; HR Tess completes the process. Track it, them, and they. Requests, dates, and approvals are pointed back to. Write in the box on this page. It stays in this browser for this visit, and it is not uploaded.

Material

About 240 words. Look up at most one unknown word. There is no word list and no full translation.

On Monday morning, Eli opens the leave form on his laptop. He fills the dates and checks them twice. His manager asks for a short coverage plan. Eli writes it in three bullets. Tess from HR reviews the form and flags two fields. Eli fixes them before noon. The manager approves the request in the system. Eli sees it turn green on his screen. Tess sends a calendar note to the team. Eli pins it above his task list. They discuss who will watch the inbox. Eli forwards two folders and labels them. On the Friday before leave he clears his desk. He locks the drawer and tests it. Tess reminds him to set an auto-reply. Eli writes it and saves a copy. While away he keeps messages unread unless they are urgent. Tess handles two vendor calls and logs them. Eli returns on a Wednesday and reads the notes. He thanks Tess and closes the leave ticket. It stays in the archive for the year. His manager asks whether the trip helped. Eli says it did and shares one photo. Tess updates the balance and shows it. Eli screenshots the number and files it. They walk to lunch and talk about next quarter. Eli puts the coverage plan back in a folder. It is ready if someone asks again. Tess marks the request complete. Eli starts the afternoon with a clear list.

Steps

  1. Read once. In Chinese, say what the main people did.
  2. Read again. For each it, them, and they, write what it points to.
  3. Answer the three questions below. Write first, then open the check.

One more round

After the three answers, tap these words back into the order of the line. About half a minute. Finish before you open the check.

Open after you write
  • writes it / sees it / pins it / tests it / writes it / closes It / says it / shows it / files it / plan It: plan, approval, calendar note, drawer, auto-reply, ticket, trip, balance, and so on
  • checks them / fixes them / labels them / logs them / unless they are: dates / fields / folders / calls / messages
  • They discuss / They walk: Eli and Tess / the people involved

Three questions

  1. The short coverage plan.
  2. The two flagged fields.
  3. The trip.

One note for Chinese speakers

In it did, it points to the trip, not the leave ticket. The manager asked whether the trip helped.

Keep one change

After the check, write only one thing to change next time.

  • If you took “it did” as the leave ticket, next time look at whether the trip helped. Change only that.
  • If they were right, write this: next time you see them or it, still find the noun first, and do not switch to copying new words.

Writing both means no focus. Keep one.
