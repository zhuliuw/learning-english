Reading · Lesson 5 · about 15 minutes

The late train

Skill
Reading
Time
About 15 minutes
Done when
You have written what each it points to, answered three questions, and written one change

This lesson is still pronouns. This time watch it. It is not always the train. Write on paper. This page does not save what you write.

Passage

This is a commute home. Look up at most one word. There is no word list, and do not translate the whole passage.

Lin Gao waits for the train home. The board above the platform changes. It says the train will come at 18:40, not 18:10. His colleague Anna stands next to him. She asks whether it is their usual train. Lin Gao says yes. It stops three times before their station. Anna takes a book from her bag and reads. She looks up when the crowd moves. The train arrives, and the doors open. Lin Gao follows Anna inside. They find two seats by the door. The ride is quiet. At his stop, Lin Gao stands up. Anna stays seated because her stop is the next one. He waves and steps onto the platform.

Steps

  1. Read once. Say in Chinese what Lin Gao is waiting for.
  2. Read again. For each it, write what it points to. You can mark she, he, and they as you go. The questions ask only about it.
  3. Answer the three questions. Write them down before you open the check.

Three questions

  1. In “It says the train will come at 18:40”, what does it point to?
  2. In “whether it is their usual train”, what does it point to?
  3. In “It stops three times”, what does it point to?
Write on paper first, then check
  • It says: the board above the platform, not the train
  • whether it is their usual train: the train
  • It stops three times: the train
  • She asks / She looks: Anna
  • They find: Lin Gao and Anna
  • He waves: Lin Gao

The questions

  1. The board above the platform.
  2. The train.
  3. The train.

Keep one change

After you check, write down one thing you will do differently.

  • If you took it in “It says” to be the train, next time look at the noun just before it: board or train. Change only that.
  • If they were right, write this: next time you meet it, still find the thing named just before it, and do not switch to copying words.

Writing both means you have no priority. Keep one.

What this lesson does not practice

Not listening, not speaking, and not turning this lesson into a word list. Reading stops at these five lessons. Writing lives under Writing, not in this lesson.
