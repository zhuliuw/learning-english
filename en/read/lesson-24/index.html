The year-end review meeting

Skill
Reading

About 20 minutes
Done when
You have written what the pronouns point to, answered three questions, and written one change

In the year-end review, Gina presents her team’s results; manager Paul asks questions. Track it, them, and they. Slides, figures, and follow-ups are pointed back to. Write in the box on this page. It stays in this browser for this visit, and it is not uploaded. Reading stops at this lesson.

Material

About 240 words. Look up at most one unknown word. There is no word list and no full translation.

On the last Friday of the year, Gina joins the review meeting with a short slide deck. Paul opens the call and shares the agenda. Gina presents three goals and explains them. She points to a chart and enlarges it. Paul asks about two delays and notes them. Gina answers and links the notes in the chat. They discuss next year’s staffing for ten minutes. Gina writes two action items and numbers them. Paul assigns owners before the call ends. Gina saves the recording link and copies it. After the meeting she sends a summary. Paul replies that it is clear. Gina prints the action list and tapes it by her monitor. It stays there through January. A teammate asks for the chart file. Gina shares it and sets view-only access. They meet again on Monday to split the work. Gina checks the owners and pings them. Paul drops by and asks whether the delays are closed. Gina says they are tracked in the list. She updates two rows and highlights them. At lunch she closes her laptop and locks it. The summary remains pinned in the team channel. Gina archives the slide deck after she backs it up. Paul thanks the team in a short note. Gina reads it and forwards it to two interns. They reply with questions about the chart. Gina answers them before the afternoon stand-up. The year ends with a clear list on her wall. She checks it once more before she leaves.

Steps

  1. Read once. In Chinese, say what the main people did.
  2. Read again. For each it, them, and they, write what it points to.
  3. Answer the three questions below. Write first, then open the check.

One more round

After the three answers, tap these words back into the order of the line. About half a minute. Finish before you open the check.

Open after you write
  • enlarges it / copies it / that it is clear / tapes It / shares it / locks it / backs it / reads it / forwards it / checks it: chart, link, summary, list, file, laptop, backup, thank-you note, and so on
  • explains them / notes them / numbers them / pings them / highlights them / answers them: goals / delays / action items / owners / rows / questions
  • They discuss / They meet / they are tracked / They reply: people in the meeting / the delays / the interns

Three questions

  1. The chart.
  2. The two delays.
  3. The delays (still).

One note for Chinese speakers

Later they are tracked points to the delays, not people. Paul asked whether the delays are closed.

Keep one change

After the check, write only one thing to change next time.

  • If you took “they are tracked” as coworkers, next time look at whether the delays are closed. Change only that.
  • If they were right, write this: next time you see them or it, still find the noun first, and do not switch to copying new words.

Writing both means no focus. Keep one.
