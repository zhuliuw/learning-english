Reading · Lesson 13 · about 20 minutes

Signing in at the community clinic

Skill
Reading
Time
About 20 minutes
Done when
You have written what the pronouns point to, answered three questions, and written one change

Ping signs in at the community clinic; neighbor Wen is in the hall. Track it, them, and they. Tickets, forms, and medicine are pointed back to. Write in the box on this page. It stays in this browser for this visit, and it is not uploaded.

Material

About 240 words. Look up at most one unknown word. There is no word list and no full translation.

On Tuesday morning, Ping takes a number at the community clinic. Wen is already sitting near the window with a folder. They nod and wait for the screen to call names. Ping’s number lights up first. She walks to desk three and shows her ID. The nurse prints a small ticket and hands it over. Ping folds it into her pocket. Wen’s turn comes ten minutes later. The doctor asks about her cough and listens to her chest. He writes two notes and signs them. Wen carries them to the pharmacy window. Ping meets her there after a blood pressure check. They compare the waiting times on their phones. A volunteer points them to the payment machine. Ping pays for her visit and keeps the receipt. She puts it next to the ticket. Wen picks up a bottle of pills and shakes it once. The pharmacist reminds her to take them after meals. Outside they share an umbrella because it starts to rain. Ping holds it while Wen locks her bike. They walk to the bus stop together. Ping gets off two stops earlier. Wen stays on and texts that the pills are sealed. At home Ping pins the receipt to a board. It stays there until she files the clinic visit in her app. She washes her hands and drinks warm water. Later Wen asks to borrow the umbrella back. Ping returns it the next morning. The ticket in her pocket is creased but still clear. She throws it away after the report appears online.

Steps

  1. Read once. In Chinese, say what the main people did.
  2. Read again. For each it, them, and they, write what it points to.
  3. Answer the three questions below. Write first, then open the check.

Three questions

One more round

After the three answers, tap these words back into the order of the line. About half a minute. Finish before you open the check.

Open after you write
  • folds it / throws it: the ticket; puts it: the receipt; shakes it: the bottle; holds it / returns it: the umbrella; It stays: the receipt
  • signs them / take them: the notes / the pills
  • They nod / They compare / They walk: Ping and Wen

Three questions

  1. The small ticket the nurse printed.
  2. The two notes the doctor wrote.
  3. The umbrella.

One note for Chinese speakers

it switches quickly: ticket, receipt, bottle, umbrella. For each one, look back to the nearest singular noun.

Keep one change

After the check, write only one thing to change next time.

  • If you took “returns it” as the bottle, next time look at borrow the umbrella. Change only that.
  • If they were right, write this: next time you see them or it, still find the noun first, and do not switch to copying new words.

Writing both means no focus. Keep one.
