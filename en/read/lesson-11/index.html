Reading · Lesson 11 · about 20 minutes

Returning books at the library

Skill
Reading
Time
About 20 minutes
Done when
You have written what the pronouns point to, answered three questions, and written one change

Qing returns books at the library and meets classmate Fang. Track it, them, and they. Books, a receipt, and seats are pointed back to. Write in the box on this page. It stays in this browser for this visit, and it is not uploaded.

Material

This is about an hour returning books, about 240 words. Look up at most one unknown word. There is no word list and no full translation.

On Saturday morning, Qing carries four books to the city library. Fang meets her at the bike rack outside. They walk in together and join a short line. Qing sets the books on the return desk. The librarian scans them one by one. One book is a day late. Qing pays a small fine and keeps the receipt. She folds it and slides it into her notebook. Fang returns two novels of her own. The librarian stamps both cards. Qing asks where the quiet study room is. The librarian points down the hall. They find two free seats by the window. Qing opens a work email on her phone and answers it. Fang reads a magazine from the rack. After twenty minutes Qing stands to stretch. She leaves her bag on the chair. Fang watches it while Qing fills a water bottle. When Qing returns, they decide to look for a history shelf. Qing pulls out a thin book and checks the first page. She puts it back because the print is too small. Fang finds a thicker book and borrows it. At the desk Qing waits while Fang signs the form. They leave before noon. Outside Qing unlocks her bike. Fang walks toward the bus stop. Qing texts her brother that the late book is returned. She puts the phone away and rides home. At home she pins the receipt to the fridge. It stays there until she checks her account next week. The bag of books by the door is empty again.

Steps

  1. Read once. In Chinese, say what the main people did.
  2. Read again. For each it, them, and they, write what it points to.
  3. Answer the three questions below. Write first, then open the check.

One more round

After the three answers, tap these words back into the order of the line. About half a minute. Finish before you open the folded check.

Open after you write
  • scans them: the four books; borrows it / puts it back: a book
  • folds it / pins the receipt / It stays: the receipt
  • watches it: the bag; They walk / they decide: Qing and Fang

Three questions

  1. Qing’s four books.
  2. The fine receipt.
  3. Qing’s bag on the chair.

One note for Chinese speakers

it here is sometimes the receipt, sometimes the bag, sometimes a book. Use the nearest singular noun.

Keep one change

After the check, write only one thing to change next time.

  • If you took “watches it” as a book, next time look back to leaves her bag. Change only that.
  • If all were right, keep this change: next time you see them or it, still find the noun first; do not switch to copying new words.

Writing both means no focus. Keep one.
