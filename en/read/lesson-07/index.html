Reading · Lesson 7 · about 20 minutes

An hour at the clinic

Skill
Reading
Time
About 20 minutes
Done when
You have marked what each pronoun points to, answered three questions, and written one change

Jun leaves work for a clinic visit; his colleague Mina has an appointment too. Track he, she, they, and it. it is sometimes the blood-pressure number, sometimes a bottle of water. Write in the box on this page. It stays in this browser for this visit, and it is not uploaded.

Material

An hour at the clinic after work, about 250 words. Look up at most one unknown word. There is no word list and no full translation.

Jun leaves the office at 18:00 and walks to the clinic two streets away. The waiting room is quiet, and a nurse calls names from a list. Jun takes a seat near the window and checks the time on his phone. His colleague Mina arrives a few minutes later for her own appointment. She sits across from him and asks whether he feels nervous. Jun says he does not. The nurse opens the door and calls Jun's name. He stands up and follows her into a small room. She checks his blood pressure and writes it on a form. The doctor comes in, asks three short questions, and looks at the form. He says the numbers look fine for now. Jun asks when he should come back. The doctor says in three months, unless something changes. Jun thanks him and walks back to the waiting room. Mina is still there with a book. She looks up when he returns. Jun says his visit was short. Mina's name is called next, and she puts the book in her bag. She waves and follows the nurse. Jun waits outside so they can walk to the station together. When Mina comes out, they leave without hurry. At the station Jun buys a bottle of water and offers it to Mina. She takes a sip and gives it back. They board the train and talk about tomorrow's meeting for a few minutes. At Jun's stop he stands up. Mina stays on because her stop is later. He nods and steps onto the platform.

Steps

  1. Read once. Say in Chinese what Jun and Mina each do.
  2. Read again. For each he, she, they, and it, write what it points to.
  3. Answer the three questions below. Write first, then open the check.

Three questions

Write all three answers below before you open the check. It stays in this browser for this visit. It is not uploaded, and it is not scored.

Write a little for each question, then open the check.

Once more

After the three answers, tap these words into the order of the line. About half a minute. Then open the check below.

After you write, open the check
  • writes it on a form: the blood-pressure number
  • offers it to Mina / gives it back: the bottle of water
  • He stands / He says / he returns / He nods: Jun
  • She sits / She looks / She waves / she puts: Mina
  • They board / they leave: Jun and Mina
  • He says the numbers / thanks him: the doctor / Jun to the doctor

The questions

  1. The blood-pressure number.
  2. The bottle of water.
  3. Jun and Mina.

One point for a Chinese speaker

it is not a person. In this passage it is sometimes the blood-pressure number on the form, sometimes the bottle of water from the station. Look at the nearest thing before it.

Keep one change

After you check, write down one thing you will do differently.

  • If you took it in “writes it” to be the form, next time look at the blood pressure just checked. Change only that.
  • If they were right, write this: next time you meet it, still find the thing just before it, and do not switch to copying words.

Writing both means you have no priority. Keep one.

What this lesson does not practice

Not listening, not speaking, and not turning this lesson into a word list. Writing lives under Writing, not in this lesson.
