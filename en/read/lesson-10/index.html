Reading · Lesson 10 · about 20 minutes

After the late shift at the store

Skill
Reading
Time
About 20 minutes
Done when
You have written what the pronouns point to, answered three questions, and written one change

Ning finishes a late supermarket shift with coworker Hao. Track it, them, and they. Lights, boxes, and keys are pointed back to. Write in the box on this page. It stays in this browser for this visit, and it is not uploaded.

Material

This is the end of a late shift, about 240 words. Look up at most one unknown word. There is no word list and no full translation.

On Thursday night, Ning locks the front doors of the supermarket at 22:00. Hao turns off half of the lights in the aisles. They still leave a few lights on near the cash desks. Ning counts the cash from the last hour and puts it in a bag. Hao wheels three empty boxes to the back room. He stacks them by the wall. Ning checks the freezer door and closes it firmly. A manager calls and asks whether the floor is mopped. Ning says yes and hangs up. Hao wipes the counter where the bread was sold. They walk the aisles one more time. Ning finds a bottle left on a shelf and returns it to the fridge. Hao picks up two plastic bags from the floor. He drops them in the recycling bin. At 22:20 they meet at the staff door. Ning holds the keys and locks it from outside. She puts the keys in a small pouch. Hao waits while she checks the pouch twice. They walk to the bus stop under a street light. The night bus comes after eight minutes. Ning taps her card and finds a seat. Hao stands near the door because the seats are full. He texts his sister that the shift is over. Ning opens a bottle of water and drinks half of it. She offers the rest to Hao, but he declines. At her stop Ning stands and says good night. Hao stays on for two more stops. Ning walks three minutes to her building. She washes her hands and puts the pouch on the table. The keys stay there until morning. She sets an alarm for the early delivery shift.

Steps

  1. Read once. In Chinese, say what the main people did.
  2. Read again. For each it, them, and they, write what it points to.
  3. Answer the three questions below. Write first, then open the check.

Three questions

Write all three below before you open the check. It stays in this browser for this visit. It is not uploaded, and it is not scored.

Write a little for all three, then open the check.

One more round

After the three answers, tap these words back into the order of the line. About half a minute. Finish before you open the folded check.

Open after you write
  • puts it / closes it / returns it / drinks half of it: cash, freezer door, bottle, or water — nearest singular noun
  • stacks them / drops them: boxes or plastic bags
  • They still leave / They walk: Ning and Hao

Three questions

  1. The cash from the last hour.
  2. The three empty boxes.
  3. The staff door.

One note for Chinese speakers

them is sometimes boxes, sometimes bags. Use the nearest plural noun. Do not treat every them as the same thing.

Keep one change

After the check, write only one thing to change next time.

  • If you took “stacks them” as the bags, next time look back to three empty boxes. Change only that.
  • If all were right, keep this change: next time you see them or it, still find the noun first; do not switch to copying new words.

Writing both means no focus. Keep one.

What this lesson does not practice
