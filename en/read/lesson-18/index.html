Reading · Lesson 18 · about 20 minutes

Signing up for the team outing

Skill
Reading
Time
About 20 minutes
Done when
You have written what the pronouns point to, answered three questions, and written one change

The team plans a weekend outing; Nora signs up in the chat, and Vic counts names. Track it, them, and they. Forms, seats, and tickets are pointed back to. Write in the box on this page. It stays in this browser for this visit, and it is not uploaded.

Material

About 240 words. Look up at most one unknown word. There is no word list and no full translation.

On Wednesday afternoon, Nora opens the team chat about the weekend outing. Vic posts a form and asks everyone to fill it by Friday. Nora reads it twice and types her name. She chooses the bus option and saves the page. Vic collects the answers and sorts them. Two seats are still free after lunch. Nora tells a new hire about them. They both confirm before the cut-off. Vic prints the name list and tapes it by the printer. Nora takes a photo of it for the group. On Saturday they meet at the gate at eight. Vic hands out tickets and counts them again. Nora finds her seat by the window. She puts her bag under it. The bus leaves on time. They reach the park before ten. Vic gathers the team for a short walk. Nora keeps the ticket stub in her pocket. It stays there until she returns the form on Monday. After the walk they share a long table for lunch. Nora pays for two drinks and passes them down. Vic takes a group photo and sends it to the chat. Nora likes it and adds a caption. On the ride back she closes her eyes for twenty minutes. Vic wakes the group when the city comes into view. They thank the driver and step off. Nora texts that the outing went well. Vic replies and closes the form. He archives it for next quarter. Nora pins the photo on her desk the next morning.

Steps

  1. Read once. In Chinese, say what the main people did.
  2. Read again. For each it, them, and they, write what it points to.
  3. Answer the three questions below. Write first, then open the check.

Three questions

One more round

After the three answers, tap these words back into the order of the line. About half a minute. Finish before you open the check.

Open after you write
  • fill it / reads it / tapes it / photo of it / under it / It stays / sends it / likes it / archives it: form, list, seat, photo, stub, and so on
  • sorts them / about them / counts them / passes them: answers / free seats / tickets / drinks
  • They both / they meet / They reach / they share / They thank: Nora and the new hire / the whole team

Three questions

  1. The form Vic posted.
  2. The two free seats.
  3. The form.

One note for Chinese speakers

Early it usually points to the form; under it points to the seat. Use the verb and the nearest noun.

Keep one change

After the check, write only one thing to change next time.

  • If you took “about them” as the answers, next time look at Two seats are still free. Change only that.
  • If they were right, write this: next time you see them or it, still find the noun first, and do not switch to copying new words.

Writing both means no focus. Keep one.
