Reading · Lesson 1 · about 20 minutes

Find who does what

Skill
Reading
Time
About 20 minutes
Done when
You have answered three questions and written one change

This is Lin Wei’s morning from the door to the end of the workday, and the report she will not finish until Thursday. Do one move: in each sentence, mark who and the verb. Do not pull the time out and make it the subject. Write on paper or in your own notes. This page does not save what you write.

Passage

This is one full work morning, in common words, about 282 words. It is long enough that you still have to see who and the verb across a whole passage. Mark them even if you already know every sentence.

Lin Wei leaves home at 7:10. She turns off the hall light and locks the door. She walks to the bus stop with a bag on her shoulder and a folded list in her coat. The bus is half empty, so she sits near the door and keeps the bag on her lap. She takes the bus and reads three short messages on the way. One message is from her manager, one is from a teammate, and one only names Thursday. She does not answer them yet. She watches the street and puts the phone back in her bag before the last stop. At the office she writes one reply before she opens her email. The reply is only two lines. She sends that reply to her manager. Then she opens the rest of the mail and sorts it into work for today and work that can wait. A colleague stops at her desk and asks about today's report. Lin Wei says no. She will finish the report on Thursday, not today. Today she only gathers the visits from Monday and Tuesday. She writes each client name, the time of the visit, and one short line. She does not add Friday. At noon she eats at her desk and reads the three morning messages again. After lunch she answers the teammate and leaves the longer question for later. By four she saves the file in the Thursday folder and writes the day on a paper note beside the keyboard. She does not promise the report in the afternoon chat. At six she shuts the laptop, puts the paper note in her bag, and leaves the office with Thursday still ahead of her.

Look up at most one word.

Steps

  1. Read once without stopping or looking words up. Then say in Chinese whose situation this is. If you cannot say it cleanly, continue anyway.
  2. Read again. In each sentence, mark who and the verb. If the verb includes not or will, mark that whole piece; do not split it. When and joins two actions, mark both. Mark both takes and reads, and mark both writes and opens.
  3. Answer the three questions. Write them down before you open the check.

Three questions

  1. What does Lin Wei read on the bus, and does she reply then?
  2. Where does she write the reply, and who is it for?
  3. When will she finish the report?

The questions

  1. She reads three short messages and does not reply yet.
  2. At the office, before she opens her email. She sends it to her manager.
  3. On Thursday, not today.

One point for a Chinese speaker

will finish means it is not done yet. on Thursday sits after the verb, not at the start. Chinese often puts the day first. Do not write Thursday she will finish.

Keep one change

After you check, write down one thing you will do differently next time.

  • If you missed a time, next time put a dot under a weekday, today, or tomorrow. Change only that.
  • If you missed the second verb after and, next time mark both actions when and joins them.
  • If the questions and the verbs were all right, write this: next lesson, still find who does what, and do not add a new job.

Writing down all three means you have no priority. Keep one.

What this lesson does not practice

Not pronunciation, not listening, and not copying this passage into a word list. This lesson ends at the check and the one change. The next lesson is the early bus, and it still only asks who does what.
