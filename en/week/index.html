Six months

For adults who already work. Six months, four weeks each. Each week, do one listening, one reading, one speaking, and one writing lesson, then rest or look at one change. Listening, speaking, and writing take about fifteen minutes. Reading takes about twenty. Speaking shadows the same audio as that week’s listening. Ticks stay on paper. This site does not remember what you checked.

If you have not read How to learn, read that page first. One lesson, one job, and one change to keep. A missed day does not need to be made up. Do month 1 first, then each month in order through month 6.

Six months

  1. Month 1Four weeks, lessons 1–4. Opening the shop, a note, a call, the market; reading covers a report, the early bus, work messages, and Saturday’s plan.
  2. Month 2Four weeks, lessons 5–8. Laundry, a meeting room, a package at the desk, a taxi after overtime; reading runs from a delayed train to a hallway water notice.
  3. Month 3Four weeks, lessons 9–12. Building maintenance, canteen lunch, pharmacy pickup, parking payment; reading runs from a bank card to a lobby delivery.
  4. Month 4Four weeks, lessons 13–16. A haircut, filling up, a new SIM, the gym; reading runs from the community clinic to the weekend market.
  5. Month 5Four weeks, lessons 17–20. Utility bills, bike repair, a health check, a passport; reading runs from laptop repair to the yearly car inspection.
  6. Month 6Four weeks, lessons 21–24. Moving day, hotel check-in, the airport, year-end invoices; reading runs from parents visiting to the year-end review.

How to use a week

Open a month, then one week, print that page, and tick it on paper. Do not put two weeks into the same evening. To swap a lesson, use the links on that week’s page, or pick another from the index.
