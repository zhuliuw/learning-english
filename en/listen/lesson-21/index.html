Listening · Lesson 21 · about 15 minutes

Loading the moving van

Skill
Listening
Time
About 15 minutes
Done when
You have said who did what in Chinese, and written one change

On moving day, Kai and the movers load the van. Do one thing: in Chinese, write who counted the boxes, what the workers lifted, who locked the old door, where the van went, and who got the keys. Do not take dictation. Write in the box on this page. It stays in this browser for this visit, and it is not uploaded.

Material

Listen first. Do not open the lines. There is no speed control. The clip is under a minute.

Write it here

In Chinese, write who did what. After you write, you can open the lines and the check. It stays in this browser for this visit. It is not uploaded, and it is not scored.

Write first, then open.

One more round

After you write, tap these words back into the order of the line. About half a minute. Finish before you open the lines below.

Open after you write

Kai counts the boxes in the hallway. Two workers lift the sofa. Kai locks the old door. The van drives to the new building. He hands the keys to the landlord.

Steps

  1. Listen once. Do not open the lines.
  2. In Chinese, say separately: who counted the boxes, what the workers lifted, who locked the door, where the van went, and who received the keys.
  3. Write first, then open the check. Only then look at the lines.

Check

Open after you write

You can say:

  • Kai counts the boxes in the hallway.
  • Two workers lift the sofa.
  • Kai locks the old door.
  • The van drives to the new building.
  • He hands the keys to the landlord.

One note for Chinese speakers

he points to Kai. The workers and the landlord are different. Do not mix locking the door and receiving the keys into one person.

Keep one change

After the check, write only one thing to change next time.

  • If you mixed locking and receiving keys, next time keep Kai locking and the landlord receiving. Change only that.
  • If the people and the actions were right, write this: next lesson, still do only one kind of task.

Writing both means no focus. Keep one.
