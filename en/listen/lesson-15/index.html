Listening · Lesson 15 · about 15 minutes

A new SIM at the shop

Skill
Listening
Time
About 15 minutes
Done when
You have said who did what in Chinese, and written one change

Jun goes to the phone shop for a new SIM. Do one thing: in Chinese, write who brought what, what the clerk checked, who signed the form, whether the card was ready, and what happened to the old card. Do not take dictation. Write in the box on this page. It stays in this browser for this visit, and it is not uploaded.

Material

Listen first. Do not open the lines. There is no speed control. The clip is under a minute.

Write it here

In Chinese, write who did what. After you write, you can open the lines and the check. It stays in this browser for this visit. It is not uploaded, and it is not scored.

Write first, then open.

One more round

After you write, tap these words back into the order of the line. About half a minute. Finish before you open the lines below.

Open after you write

Jun brings his ID to the phone shop. The clerk checks his old number. Jun signs the transfer form. The new SIM works on the first try. He cuts the old card in half.

Steps

  1. Listen once. Do not open the lines.
  2. In Chinese, say separately: who brought ID, what the clerk checked, who signed, whether the new SIM worked, and what happened to the old card.
  3. Write first, then open the check. Only then look at the lines.

Check

Open after you write

You can say:

  • Jun brings his ID to the phone shop.
  • The clerk checks his old number.
  • Jun signs the transfer form.
  • The new SIM works on the first try.
  • He cuts the old card in half.

One note for Chinese speakers

he points to Jun. The clerk is separate. Do not mix checking the number and signing into one person.

Keep one change

After the check, write only one thing to change next time.

  • If you mixed checking and signing, next time keep the clerk checking and Jun signing. Change only that.
  • If the people and the actions were right, write this: next lesson, still do only one kind of task.

Writing both means no focus. Keep one.
